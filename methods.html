How Analog Atlas works

Analog sites let mission teams rehearse rovers, drills, habitats and crews before they fly. Today they are chosen mostly by experience — Devon Island, the Atacama, the Dry Valleys. Analog Atlas makes that choice quantitative, global, transparent and reproducible. It turns ~30 open Earth-observation layers into a ranking of every land cell on Earth against a specific Moon-base region or Mars landing site and a specific mission objective. It then checks that ranking against the analog sites agencies already use.

1 · Pipeline at a glance

Everything runs as a static website. Scoring 88,648 cells takes about 0.1 s in the browser, so every slider re-ranks the whole planet live. Nothing is precomputed except the feature grid and the published validation numbers, and both are rebuilt by scripts in the repository.

2 · Earth data inventory

All climate variables are long-term climatologies from NASA POWER, read directly from its AWS Open Data bucket (s3://nasa-power, Zarr). Grids are resampled to 0.5° cells. A cell is kept when at least 25% of it is land in the GLOBE 1 km mask, which keeps volcanic islands such as Lanzarote and Hawaii.

3 · Planetary targets

The app contains 23 targets. The 13 Moon targets cover NASA's nine Artemis III south-pole candidate regions (Oct 2024, centres averaged from the landing sites in JGR Planets 2025) plus the 2022 Connecting Ridge region. They also include ISRO's Chandrayaan-3 Shiv Shakti point, the Mare Tranquillitatis and Marius Hills lava-tube skylights, and Apollo 15 Hadley Rille. The 10 Mars targets are Jezero, Gale, Arcadia Planitia, Utopia/Zhurong, Deuteronilus Mensae, Meridiani, Hellas, the Tharsis lava plains, Valles Marineris and the Phoenix site.

Each target records surface temperature mean and extremes, diurnal range, pressure, albedo, maximum Sun elevation, typical slope, geology tags, ice context and radiation dose, each with sources and a confidence flag. Measured values come from LRO Diviner/LOLA, Chandrayaan-3 ChaSTE, Chang'e-4 LND, Curiosity REMS/RAD, Perseverance MEDA, Zhurong, Phoenix and SHARAD/SWIM. The data are in data/targets.json, and each source is linked from the target card in the app.

4 · Scoring model

4.1 Translating a planetary condition into an Earth ideal

No place on Earth is −120 °C or airless. For each feature we therefore define the ideal Earth value as the planetary value clamped to Earth's attainable envelope (the 1st–99th percentile over land cells). Then “as cold as Earth gets”, “as dry as Earth gets” or “as thin an atmosphere as Earth has” become concrete targets. Lighting is not looked up but computed. For any body and latitude we integrate solar elevation over a year using that body's obliquity (Moon 1.54°, Mars 25.19°, Earth 23.44°). That gives the fraction of daylight with the Sun below 10° and the fraction of the year in polar night. The same code is used for Earth's cells, so lunar polar lighting maps onto Earth's high latitudes on equal terms.

4.2 Similarity in percentile space

Let Fj be Earth's empirical distribution (CDF) of feature j over all land cells. The similarity of cell i to the ideal x*j is

sij = ( 1 − | Fj(xij) − Fj(x*j) | )k,   k = 3

In words: how much of Earth's land lies between this site and the target condition? The measure needs no units or hand-tuned tolerances, and it is robust to heavy-tailed variables such as precipitation and relief. We first tried a Gaussian kernel in physical units. It failed for targets beyond Earth's envelope because only the Antarctic interior could score; the percentile kernel fixed this. Geology layers (distance to volcanic edifices, impact structures, playas or glaciers) use a distance-decay kernel instead:

s = 1 / (1 + (d / L)²),   L = 40–60 km

4.3 Six factor groups, non-compensatory aggregation

Features are averaged within six groups: thermal regime, aridity & biosphere, atmosphere & radiation, terrain, geology & ice, and polar lighting. Each feature carries a relevance rj set by the target, for example 0.15 for UV at grazing-Sun polar sites. The groups are combined with a weighted geometric mean:

Gg(i) = Σj∈g rj sij / Σj∈g rj     S(i) = Πg max(0.02, Gg(i))wg/Σw · γ(i)

Group weights are wg = objective weight × target-category emphasis × mean feature relevance. The geometric mean is non-compensatory: a hot desert cannot make up for being 70 °C too warm for a lunar polar target by being very dry. A plain weighted sum (the classic GIS weighted linear combination) is available as a toggle for comparison. It reaches a similar AUC, but it ranks the Sahara above the Arctic for −100 °C polar targets, which is physically wrong. γ is a substrate gate: targets that need exposed regolith or rock penalise ice-sheet cover above the target's own ice ideal (up to ×0.15). Ice cover is the Natural Earth glacier mask multiplied by CERES albedo, which keeps dark ice-free oases such as the McMurdo Dry Valleys.

Every number in a site report is reproducible: explainCell() returns each feature's raw value, its Earth percentile, the target condition, its match and its weight. The test-suite checks that this explanation sums back to the map score.

5 · Mission objectives

Category emphasis multiplies these weights. For example, south-pole targets stress thermal ×1.4 and lighting ×1.6, lava-tube targets stress geology ×1.6, and canyons stress terrain ×1.5. Users can also set all six weights by hand.

6 · Validation

A ranking is only useful if it agrees with expert experience. We assembled 36 established analog field sites from NASA, ESA, CSA, ISRO, CNSA, OeWF and the Mars Society, with coordinates, themes, programmes and sources (data/analogs.json). For each target, the positives are the sites whose themes match its category: polar/ice sites for the lunar south pole and Mars ground ice, volcanic/lava-tube sites for maria and pits, and arid/evaporite sites for Mars sedimentary basins. The score is the ROC AUC against all land cells, i.e. the probability that a matched known analog outranks a random land cell.

Negative controls (Amazon, Congo Basin, Paris, Singapore) rank below the 50th percentile for Mars targets, and this is enforced in tests/engine.test.mjs. A regression guard fails the build if the mean AUC drops below 0.72. Sites that score low tell us something too. Process analogs (Rio Tinto's acid chemistry, Pavilion Lake's microbialites) are chosen for chemistry that no climate layer captures. Small peaks and islands (Mauna Kea, Teide) are averaged with their humid lowlands at 0.5°.

7 · Robustness & novelty

Robustness. Weights are a judgement call, so for every candidate we run a Monte-Carlo test in a Web Worker. Each run perturbs every group weight and the kernel exponent by up to ±35%, re-ranks the whole planet and picks the top 12 distinct regions again. The reported rank stability is the fraction of 24 runs in which the candidate's region is still among them. A highly robust candidate is an analog under almost any reasonable weighting.

Novelty. The challenge asks for new analogs, so every cell carries its distance to the nearest established site. Candidates more than 250 km from all of them (adjustable) are flagged NEW. The Discoveries tab lists, for every target, the best sites more than 300 km from any known analog. Some recur across many targets: the Chersky Range (Yakutia), the Pamir and Kunlun, the Prince Charles Mountains (Antarctica), Baffin Island, and the Tibesti and Ahaggar volcanic massifs. Accessibility is the distance to the nearest airport, town of ≥5,000 people or Antarctic station, and it can be used as a filter.

8 · Limitations (read before planning a field trip)

  • Resolution. A 0.5° cell is about 50 × 55 km. Analog value often lives at the outcrop scale: a single lava tube, a crater rim or a salt pan. Analog Atlas is a regional screening tool that says where to look; finer data and fieldwork must follow.
  • Reanalysis climate. MERRA-2 is a model-assimilation product. It is excellent for regional climatology but smooths mountains and coastal gradients.
  • Geology is proxied. We use distance to mapped volcanoes, impact structures and playas. We do not yet use mineralogy (basalt vs anorthosite, sulfates, phyllosilicates) or grain size.
  • Planetary values carry uncertainty. Several south-pole temperatures and Mars site values are literature estimates, flagged medium or low confidence on each target card.
  • Validation set. 36 sites with uneven theme coverage; AUC per target uses 9–21 positives. The known analogs were chosen partly for logistics, not only fidelity.
  • Ice mask. Natural Earth paints Antarctica as fully glaciated, and the CERES-albedo correction is a coarse fix for ice-free oases.

9 · Roadmap

  • Higher resolution from NASA Earthdata: MODIS LST/NDVI at 1 km, SRTM/Copernicus 30 m DEM, and Landsat or ASTER for regolith colour and texture. The scoring engine already works at any grid size.
  • Mineralogy from NASA EMIT and ASTER spectral indices, matched against CRISM (Mars) and M³/Kaguya (Moon) mineral maps.
  • Planet-side maps. Score not only Earth against a target but whole LOLA/Diviner and MOLA/TES grids, so any point on the Moon or Mars can become a target.
  • Community feedback loop. Field teams rate visited candidates, and their ratings refine the weights.

10 · Reproduce it

pip install xarray zarr s3fs h5netcdf netCDF4 scipy pillow
python pipeline/fetch.py   # ~270 MB of open inputs into pipeline/.cache
python pipeline/build.py  # → data/earth.bin + data/earth.json + textures
node tools/evaluate.mjs   # → data/validation.json + data/discoveries.json
node --test tests/*.test.mjs
python3 -m http.server   # open http://localhost:8000
